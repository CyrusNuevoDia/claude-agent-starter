*FOIA / case sourcing criteria*

1\. Qualification Criteria (the 3 filters)

***1\. Story depth*** \- is there a real narrative arc (buildup, confrontation, resolution), not just a clip

As you can see in this article, there is no bodycam shown in the news snippet (meaning we cannot verify bodycam quality), it is a DUI case (avoid unless there are other crazy elements), as well as the police interaction part of the article is uninteresting and boring.

	\- What to look for:  
[https://www.wpbf.com/article/florida-boynton-beach-police-trooper-dragged-traffic-stop/71539275](https://www.wpbf.com/article/florida-boynton-beach-police-trooper-dragged-traffic-stop/71539275)

The main thing I see in this article is just the craziness of the case, it doesn't show a bodycam in the news snippet but again it's not a mandatory thing, what I saw also was the detail of what happened in the article:

The person fled the traffic stop, he dragged a state trooper, they found a shirt with blood stains on it, and in general had very interesting and detailed interaction with the police, so interesting details that are crazy, and then build a story, is what we are looking for.

* ***3\. Bodycam quality*** \- clear video/audio:

  There are some no-brainer guidelines that makes footage useable, and here is some of them:

  \- If the footage is night-time, does it have decent lighting?  
  \- Is the video showing suspect/something interesting on the screen?  
  \- Is the video actually watchable without being majorly disturbed? (Visually test this)

  A lot of bodycams vary and some can be decent quality without being super high resolution, so a lot of this process needs to be intuition, but, ill give a few examples here:

2\. Disqualifiers

* No PD/jurisdiction to file the FOIA request against (There most of the time is)  
* Footage already covered by competitor channels   
* Low severity, no real conflict or stakes  
* Audio/video quality too poor to build a full video around

3\. Workflow Once Qualified

1. Researcher sources and logs candidate cases  
2. Cases are reviewed and approved (**Ethan**)  
3. Approved cases are submitted as formal FOIA requests to the relevant PD (Follow up as seen fit)  
4. PD returns an invoice for the records request  
5. Case is re-verified for quality/fit before payment is authorized (**Henry**)  
6. Invoice is paid  
7. Bodycam footage is received (turnaround varies by PD)  
8. If footage is usable and meets quality standards, the video is produced and posted (Incentives paid out)
